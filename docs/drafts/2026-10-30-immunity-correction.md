## Why change it

The current revision exercise asks learners to place each term into exactly one of barrier, innate response, acquired response or prevention. Those headings mix categories: skin and mucus form part of innate protection, while vaccination is a preventive measure that usually aims to establish an active adaptive response. A single mutually exclusive column can therefore punish a valid relationship.

The current image (`public/images/biology/immunity-barrier-memory-v1.png`) stacks a skin layer directly on an airway-like ciliated epithelium. Its “Cilia” leader ends at a cell body rather than the apical projections. Arrows between barriers, innate response, acquired response and vaccination can also imply one compulsory sequence; vaccination is a distinct way to initiate an adaptive response, not a stage that necessarily follows infection. The image should be removed from the draft pending a separately reviewed original replacement. No new image has been produced here.

## Proposed replacement: opening explanation

> The body uses several interacting forms of protection. Skin and the mucus-covered surfaces of the respiratory tract help restrict entry; they are components of innate defence, not a separate alternative to it. Other innate mechanisms include cells and soluble factors that can act without prior exposure to a particular antigen. Adaptive immunity involves antigen-specific responses and can form immunological memory. These systems interact, so the headings describe related functions rather than isolated stages that must occur in a fixed order.
>
> Active and passive describe how specific protection is acquired. In active immunity, the person's own immune system responds after exposure to an antigen, including through vaccination. In passive immunity, ready-made antibodies are supplied from another source. Passive transfer provides antibodies without itself training the recipient to make antigen-specific memory cells.

## Proposed replacement: study task

> Draw a two-level map. Put **innate** and **adaptive** as two branches of immune defence. Under innate, place skin, mucus and an innate immune cell. Under adaptive, distinguish an active response from the receipt of ready-made antibodies. Now add vaccination beside the map as a preventive measure that can produce active adaptive immunity. Explain why skin belongs under both “barrier” and “innate”, and why “prevention” answers a different question from “which immune mechanism?”.
>
> Check your map against the sources. Use arrows to show relationships, and write one sentence explaining what the receipt of antibodies does not itself establish about the recipient's memory cells.

This task is independently written and uses no reproduced official questions or options. The explanatory mapping is supplied here for review, not hidden as a graded answer bank.

## Proposed figure direction

Replace the composite with two clearly separated, independently drawn panels only if a figure adds learning value:

1. **Skin barrier:** intact external surface. Do not put mucus or cilia within skin.
2. **Airway surface:** label airway lumen, mucus, apical cilia and epithelium. Show cilia projecting from the epithelial surface and mucus above the cells. If showing a movement arrow, identify it as mucus transport and make its direction clear.

Use an accompanying text explanation rather than presenting vaccination as a mandatory fourth stage. A diagram is a schematic, not a histology image; verify every pointer and caption. Any artwork must be original, versioned and not copied or traced from a source. The current image is not overwritten.
