# Dihybrid probability learning brief

Prepared 2026-10-12 for the English MedQGo library.

Learning outcome: derive a two-locus offspring probability from the actual parental genotypes, selecting multiplication for independent requirements and addition for mutually exclusive routes.

NCERT anchor: Class 12 Biology Chapter 4, Principles of Inheritance and Variation, inheritance of two genes / independent assortment. Official PDF: https://ncert.nic.in/textbook/pdf/lebo104.pdf . Supplementary checks: OpenStax Biology 2e 12.2 and 12.3, gamete construction, forked-line probabilities, genotype/phenotype separation and linkage boundary.

Closest guides inspected: mendelian-inheritance (single-locus 1:2:1 and 3:1), incomplete-dominance-and-codominance (heterozygote expression), linkage-and-recombination-frequency (phase and a 16% testcross record). New primary learning path: factor a two-locus cross into locus-specific distributions, combine requirements, then regroup outcomes. No cross-frequency estimation, heterozygote-colour narrative or repeated single-locus overview.

Relationships to keep separate: segregation vs assortment; gamete frequency vs offspring frequency; genotype vs phenotype; mutually exclusive alternatives vs overlapping events. Confusion checks: Aa is not a gamete at locus A; AaBb does not imply a 9:3:3:1 cross; A_ is a phenotype grouping under complete dominance; independent gametes must not be assumed for linked markers.

Original assets: six-label probability tree with four gamete endpoints; comparison table of the same requested phenotype classes under three different crosses. Worked problem uses AaBb x Aabb with 64 hypothetical offspring, followed by a genotype-specific target. Recall task changes just the second parent and checks normalisation.

Links planned to three verified live authored guides: Mendelian inheritance as prerequisite, linkage frequency as boundary/follow-on, incomplete dominance as phenotype-rule comparison. Original examples, prose and figures; no source questions or artwork reproduced. Clinical genetics and statistical hypothesis testing excluded.
